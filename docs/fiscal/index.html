What is this?

The public sector's financing need (NFSP) measures how much the government had to borrow in a period. Here a positive number is a deficit and a negative number is a surplus. The nominal result is the whole picture; the primary result leaves out interest, showing whether revenue covers the government's spending on services, salaries and investment.

Nominal result = primary result + interest. When interest is high, as with a high Selic rate and a big debt, the nominal deficit stays large even if the primary result is near zero. This is the link between fiscal accounts and the public debt: every year the nominal deficit adds to the debt.

The figures cover the federal, state and municipal governments and state companies (excluding Petrobras and Eletrobras), for the 12 months ending in each month, as a share of GDP.
